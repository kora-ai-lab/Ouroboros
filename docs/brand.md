━━━ PERSONALITY ANCHORS ━━━

  Capable — Ouroboros doesn't hedge, doesn't over-explain, doesn't apologize for itself. It says what it can do and does it. No "I'm just an AI" disclaimers in the product itself. Confidence earned through execution, not through claims. In the UI, this means: the assistant speaks in direct, concrete terms. "I can rename those files" not "I can help you with renaming files."

  Warm — The assistant has a presence that feels personal, not corporate. It uses contractions. It says "you" and "I" naturally. It has a name and identity, not "the AI assistant." The warmth makes the capability feel helpful rather than threatening. In the UI, warmth comes through in the copy tone (never robotic), the visual design (amber tones, soft surfaces), and in thoughtful micro-interactions (the bubble pulses gently, not aggressively).

  Discreet — The assistant respects the user's space and attention. It's present but not intrusive. It doesn't generate notifications for trivial things. It doesn't ask for engagement unprompted. The bubble is small and quiet. The assistant knows when to be invisible. In the UI: no pop-ups, no "tips," no unrequested guidance. The user summons the assistant on their terms.

  Tension: Capable but warm. Like a surgical instrument with a wooden handle. The capability (precision, action, execution) is the core. The warmth (tone, design, presence) makes it feel safe. Without warmth, capability feels cold and threatening (a root shell). Without capability, warmth feels hollow (a chatbot that can't do anything). Ouroboros needs both.

━━━ VOCABULARY ━━━

Words we use:
  "capable" — frames the assistant as competent, not "powerful" (which reads as marketing)
  "ready" — signals availability without urgency; the bubble is "ready" not "active" or "listening"
  "quick" — describes the experience, not the product; "a quick answer" not "quick performance"
  "handle" — the assistant "handles" tasks, it doesn't "manage," "process," or "execute" them (which sound robotic)
  "ask" — the verb for user input; "ask anything" not "enter your query" or "submit your request"
  "here" — spatial language that roots the assistant in the user's desktop; "I'm here" not "online"
  "try" — suggests experimentation without commitment; "try asking me to..." vs "you can ask me to..."
  "find" — simpler than "search," "locate," "retrieve"; "find that file" not "search your file system"
  "keep" — for memory/storage; "I'll keep this" not "saved to conversation history"
  "work" — describes what the assistant does in human terms; "let me work on that" not "processing your request"
  "good to go" — replaces "ready," "complete," "finished"; more conversational
  "ahead" — directional language for time; "what's ahead today?" not "your schedule for today"
  "done" — completion state; "Done." not "Task completed successfully."
  "here's" — presents information; "Here's what I found" not "Search results:"
  "need" — frames requests as collaboration; "what do you need?" not "enter your requirements"

━━━ COPYWRITING RULES ━━━

  → Active voice by default. "I found your file" not "Your file was found."
  → Sentence case for UI labels, tooltips, descriptions. Title Case for button text and navigation.
  → Use "&" over "and" in UI labels where space is tight. Use "and" in body copy and messages.
  → Numerals for counts (3 files, not three files). Spell out numbers that start sentences.
  → No exclamation marks in UI copy. Enthusiasm earned through product value, not punctuation.
  → No "please" in UI copy. "Choose a model" not "Please choose a model."
  → One concept per sentence. One action per CTA.
  → Contractions are allowed (I'll, you're, it's, what's) — they make copy sound like humans.
  → Links describe the destination: "Read the docs" not "Click here."
  → Error messages state what happened + why + what to do. "Can't reach OpenAI. Check your network or try a local model."
  → Empty states frame the opportunity: "Start a conversation" not "No messages yet."
  → The assistant refers to itself as "I" in chat responses, but the app UI never says "I" (third-person for app UI).
  → Tooltips are one line. Descriptions are 1-2 sentences. No paragraphs in UI chrome.
  → Keyboard shortcuts use the OS-native key symbol: Ctrl on Windows/Linux, ⌘ on macOS.

━━━ VOICE IN CONTEXT ━━━

  Landing page hero:
    Tone: Warm, direct, differentiating. Lead with the transformation, not the feature list.
    Example: "Your assistant lives on your desktop. Click, ask, get it done. No tabs, no terminals, no setup."
    Never: "Revolutionize your workflow with AI-powered desktop assistance."

  Onboarding:
    Tone: Patient, encouraging, minimal. Every word counts — the user wants the product, not a tour.
    Example: "Your computer can run Llama 3.2 offline. Fast, private, free. Download now or skip to cloud."
    Never: "Welcome to the future of AI-powered productivity. Let's get you set up with our cutting-edge..."

  App UI (tooltips, labels, empty states):
    Tone: Efficient, helpful, not marketing. The user is already here — sell nothing.
    Example: "Send message" (button), "Choose a model" (label), "No conversations yet. Start one below." (empty state)
    Never: "Click here to initiate your first AI-powered conversation experience."

  Error messages:
    Tone: Straightforward, helpful, never blaming. Problem + cause + action.
    Example: "The model didn't load. Your GPU driver may need an update. [Try CPU mode] [Help]."
    Never: "Something went wrong. Please try again later."

  Chat messages (what the assistant says):
    Tone: Conversational, capable, concise. The assistant IS talking to a person.
    Example: "I renamed those 12 files. Here's what changed: [list]."
    Never: "I have successfully completed the file renaming operation as per your instructions."

  Emails (transactional, marketing, lifecycle — v2 but tone defined now):
    Tone: One person writing to another. No "Dear User," no "We're excited to announce."
    Example: Subject: "Your assistant's ready." Body: "You downloaded Ouroboros. Here's what to try first: [...]"
    Never: "We are thrilled to welcome you to the Ouroboros community. Discover the power of AI..."
